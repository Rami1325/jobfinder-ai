### Deterministic routes stay free — and the `/tools/ats-scan` cautionary tale

Written in Phase 22; code comments cite it as "the `/tools/ats-scan` cautionary tale in CLAUDE.md".

- **Deterministic routes stay uncapped, uncharged and smoke-pinned**: `/render`, `/render/pages` (the PDF as page pictures for a phone, PLAN 31.2/4), `/tools/review`, `/tools/ats-xray`, `/tools/page-count`, `/tools/coverage`. **`/tools/coverage` AND `/tools/review` take `jd: JDModel`, never `jd_text`** — a route that accepted job-ad text would have to reach the model to use it, and an uncapped door onto the model is the thing that rule exists to prevent. Both pinned at 422.
  `/tools/ats-scan` broke that rule until 22.10 and is the case worth remembering — **the route and its module were deleted in Phase 28, and `/tools/review` inherited the lesson**: it carried **no `Depends` at all** while `jd_text` flowed into `analyze_jd`, so anyone past the shared access-code gate spent the account's OpenAI budget with no cap and no token row — thirty lines above the docstring stating the rule. Its guard could not fail in either direction: it posted `jd_text: ""`, the one value that never enters the branch, under a label asserting the route calls no model. Behaviour alone still cannot pin this (a re-introduced analyser also returns 200, there being no cap to trip), so the **shape** (`jd_text` → 422, via `extra="forbid"`) and the **source** (the module is grepped for the analyser and the client factory) are pinned too. `extra="forbid"` is load-bearing on its own: without it a stale caller gets a silent format-only scan and a coverage number that quietly became zero.

### Phase 30 — login first, one monthly pool, and Continue with Google (2026-09-16)

The owner asked for three things: "all the buttons that let the user use the app first ask him to login or signup", "10 functions per month for free users", and Google sign-in, reversing Phase 29's deferral. What shipped: login-first entry points, the CV scan moved inside the app, one shared monthly pool with session passes and a fit-check ride, alerts charging, Continue with Google, and a verify link that finishes on any device. An adversarial five-dimension review followed; every fix below is smoke-pinned or check-mirrors-pinned with its false-positive half beside it.

*(The sign-in half of this phase — "No feature is anonymous any more", Continue with Google, the verify link, and
the `MAX_NEXT` / `GOOGLE_FAIL_PER_IP` knobs — is in `accounts-and-auth.md`.)*

**The pool belongs to the PERSON, not the account** (owner decision: closing an account does not reset the month).
- **`quota_key` is a keyed hash of the CANONICAL sign-in address** (`canonical_email` lower-cases, cuts the local part at a `+`, and drops Gmail's dots), so deleting the data, closing the account and signing up again with the same address are ONE pool. That is Phase 29's A7 rule — "closing cannot reset a limit" — applied to the free limit. An account with no sign-in address (the admin, an invite code) is its own pool, `u:<id>`, which the surviving user row preserves just the same.
- **It is derived at RESERVE time from `login.email`, never stamped at signup**, because an unverified login can still change its address and the pool must follow the address it verifies; and never keyed on the Google `sub`, so linking Google mid-month changes nothing.
- **The three tables are never wiped** — see the `_wipe_user_rows` rule in `data-and-privacy.md`, whose carve-out is AST-pinned by smoke 32.10 rather than left to a reader's care.
- **A pool is a hash, so rotating `AUTH_SECRET` re-keys every pool and resets this month's counts.** It is recorded beside the throttles it already re-keys, and it is the one knob whose side effect is invisible from the outside.

**Where the charge goes, and in what order — both learned from what the alternative would bill.**
- **Charges run INSIDE the handler, never in a dependency.** FastAPI resolves sub-dependencies before body validation, so a dependency charge would bill a malformed-body 422 — a use spent on a request that never reached a model.
- **Daily cap FIRST, monthly reserve second.** A daily 429 must never spend a monthly use, and a daily count is never refunded, so the cheap refusal has to come first. The consequence is recorded rather than hidden: a monthly 429 that follows the daily count has still spent the daily units.
- **A `charged(...)` block sits OUTSIDE the handler's `try/except → 502`**, or the 429 it raises comes back as a 502 and the user is told the server broke when it was a limit.
- **Every failure is refunded for now** (owner decision, to revisit with paid tiers), whether the error is ours or OpenAI's.
- **A refund lands in the CHARGE's own month; the header reads the CURRENT one.** Both halves are needed and the second was a review fix: a fit check at 23:00Z on 30 September with its covered tailor at 00:30Z on 1 October wrote the ledger correctly into September and then sent `X-Uses-Remaining` from September's exhausted count, so the client disabled every counted button while October was untouched. The ledger write keeps the charge's period, `_note_remaining` reads `period_of(now)`. The invariant either side of it is `SUM(delta) == used` per (pool, month), and a refund is idempotent because a charge event's `refunded` can only grow up to its `delta`.

**A session is one use, not one call per message.**
- **`pass_charged` opens a PASS**: interview practice 3 hours / 60 calls, screening answers 3 hours / 6 answers, a cover letter 24 hours / 10 calls keyed by the analysed JD. The screening window is what the Chrome extension's autofill rides — it fires up to 4 answers per click, sends no grouping id, and is installed software a deploy cannot change.
- **The ride is one conditional UPDATE narrowed by `max(id)`, with its conditions repeated OUTSIDE the subquery and never an `ORDER BY … LIMIT`**: Postgres re-checks only the outer WHERE after a lock wait, so the inner conditions alone would let two callers ride one slot.
- **Opening re-runs the ride under the month row's own lock before deciding the 429**, so at 9 of 10 used, five concurrent first calls spend 1 use and none is refused.
- **A pass keeps its use once ANY call on it succeeded.** Refunding a late opener failure would re-open a free ride (force the opener to fail, then ride for nothing); a pass on which nothing succeeded is refunded once and closed, so an outage that fails every call in a session costs nothing. A failed ride still keeps its slot.
- **`POST /tailor/stream` charges exactly as `/tailor` does** (PLAN 31.3/2): the daily tailor cap, then the fit ride or one reserved use, all BEFORE the stream starts, so a refusal is a plain HTTP 429 with the uses header; the use is reserved last, right before the request's session closes (the search stream's rule). The worker settles on a short session of its own: a served tailor settles the ride (the user row reloaded there, since the request's copy is detached) or keeps the use, and a failed one releases the ride or refunds the use BEFORE its error frame is queued. Classed `charged:tailor` in 32.13 and driven in its sweep, and 32.13(b)'s list of fit-ride routes names it beside `/jobs/fit` and `/tailor`. Smoke: the stages in order and one result, the use kept, a model failure and a size refusal each one error frame with the use given back, a fit check's ride claimed, and a failed covered stream giving the ride back.
- **The job page's letter rides the same pass** (PLAN 31.4/4). The letter left the tailor drawer for the job's own page, whose `CoverLetter` is keyed by the analysis stored on the row (`jd_json`), the one the draft was tailored against, so a pass opened on either page covers the other. Writing it on a row with no stored analysis first reads the posting through `/jd/analyze` (the daily `jd_analyze` count, never a monthly use) and stores it; the card then states the letter's price before anything is spent. `GET /applications/{id}/review`, which reopens a saved draft, reaches no model and is classed `free` in 32.13 and driven in its sweep.
- **Since PLAN 31.3/1 it is the only way in**, and a reading taken beside a tailored draft is held in the dialog and adopted by the page at Tailor, so that tailor claims its ride too (`document-editor.md`). The line before a reading is `uses.fitThenTailor`, which prices the one button and says what it buys; `uses.fitOrTailor`, which priced two buttons side by side, is gone.
- **Check fit, then Tailor the same posting, costs 1 use.** The fit check opens a 24-hour, one-call ride keyed by the analysed JD it returned — keyed by the JD because `TailorRequest` carries no job text to key on. A tailor that raises RELEASES the ride rather than settling it, so the retry is still covered; a success writes `fit_check −1` and `tailor +1` in the fit's own month, leaving `used` unchanged while the breakdown counts a tailored job.
- **`/jobs/match` refuses more than 10 listings with a 400 before any call.** One use covered a serial loop of two model calls per listing and the list had no maximum, so one use bought any number of fit readings.
- **What one pass call can send, and what that does NOT bound (P30-PASS-SIZE, 2026-09-21).** Every input a pass call puts in the prompt is measured now (`llm-boundary.md`, *Every builder parameter is measured or named*): the resume 256 KB, the posting as text 32 KB or as the analysed JD 128 KB, a question or an answer 16 KB, the interview transcript 256 KB (the scorecard 288 KB). So the widest call on any pass is the interview scorecard, which sends at most `max_resume_kb + max_jd_kb + max_scorecard_transcript_kb`, about 576 KB of client text; a chat turn sends at most `max_resume_kb + max_jd_kb + max_transcript_kb`, about 544 KB, and every other pass call at most about 400 KB (a model answer: the resume, the analysed JD and a question) — or the model's context window, if that is smaller. **The context window is not a ceiling this repo can count on**: production runs `gpt-5.4-mini` (SENIOR_REVIEW.md, P6; `vercel env pull` cannot confirm it, because it returns every Sensitive variable, `MODEL_ID` included, as `""`), whose window the repo does not record, and the documented step-up `gpt-4.1-mini` has 1M tokens — on any window past 128k the checked resume and job ad (an estimated 70-120k tokens at their caps, not measured) cannot fill it, so it was the unmeasured pass inputs (the transcript, the analysed JD, a question, an answer) that set the per-call ceiling before this change, and it is these caps that set it now. **This bounds how much text one call can send. It is NOT a cost bound on a pass**: one interview use is still up to 60 such calls in 3 hours, of which the daily `llm` cap of 150 bounds how many happen, and no dollar figure for that worst case has been measured on the deployed model. Narrowing the pass itself — fewer calls, or a per-pass token budget, either of which means a heavy honest session can cost two uses — is the owner's product decision, not a technical one.
- **A size refusal on a pass input is raised INSIDE the pass**, like the resume's in 32.6: an opener gives its use back, a ride keeps its slot. Checking before `pass_charged` would save one ride slot and no model spend (the builder refuses before any call either way) at the cost of a deliberate, pinned assertion. The shape refusals — a `ChatTurn.role` that is not one of the two, a cover-letter `tone` over 200 characters — are 422s, raised before the handler, so they never reach a pass.

**A remounted page reads its posting's pass back (P30-RELOAD-PASS, 2026-09-21).**
- **A per-posting pass is listed nowhere a remount can read it.** `/auth/me` lists only `LISTED_PASSES`, and `_note_pass` sends no header for a pass with a ref. So the cover-letter pass lived in `CoverLetter`'s component state and nowhere else. Remounting the card forgot it: a reload of `/kits/:id` (whose JD the server stores, so the pass stays redeemable) or Tracker and back on `/app`. At 0 uses left, `disabled={uses.out}` then refused a change the server would still include. Reproduced in the browser at 390 px before the fix. After it, the reloaded kit read "Changes included · 9 left" with Generate enabled, in both locales, and another posting at zero still read "No uses left" with Generate disabled. "Errs toward stating a cost" was true only above zero. At zero the error was refusing the call, which is the one error `usesStore`'s header forbids.
- **`POST /cover-letter/pass` reads it back, and takes nothing.**
  - `quota.posting_pass` selects the row `_newest_open` names, which is the row `_take_pass` would take. It writes nothing: no commit, no prune, no header.
  - It takes `{jd}` only (`extra="forbid"`). `jd_text`, or a `resume` beside the JD, is a 422.
  - The JD is hashed with the letter's own `jd_ref`, so there is one key and one answer.
  - It reads the caller's own rows only. It runs on plain `current_user`, is uncapped and uncharged, is classed `free` in 32.13 and is driven in its sweep.
  - It raises `ValueError` for anything but a per-posting pass, for an empty ref and for a naive clock. So it can never become a reader of fit rides, or of the passes `/auth/me` already lists.
  - It answers the same `UsagePassOut` that `/auth/me` uses, which 32(j) compares whole.
  - Smoke 32.20 pins it: the catch beside a no-take twin, agreement with the next ride, and a used-up pass (a 429 at zero, a new +1 pass with uses left). Also a stranger, another posting and the admin, each reading 0/0, and the window on an injected clock.
- **Both answers carry relative seconds.**
  - The letter's response now has `expires_in_s` beside `included_until`. It is read as the response is built, after the model call, not when the slot was taken, so the model call's latency is not added to the time left. `included_until` stays for a tab loaded before `expires_in_s` existed.
  - `usesStore.inclusionFrom` turns either answer into a deadline on arrival (`setUsage`'s clock rule). A phone whose clock runs ahead read the absolute instant as over, and at 0 uses left that disabled a covered change.
  - `CoverLetter` never reads `included_until`, anywhere in the file, and check-mirrors 36 pins that.
- **Until the card knows, it states no cost and disables nothing.**
  - The card asks on mount, keyed on the posting AND on `limited`. It asks only when a monthly limit is known, so the admin never pays the round trip. Without `limited` in the deps, a card that mounted before the count was known would never ask once it was, and would stay unknown for good.
  - Each probe starts by setting `known` to false, so a new posting never borrows the last posting's answer.
  - `known` starts false (`useState(false)`). While the probe is pending or has failed, `known` is false. There is no `UsesNote`, which would print "No uses left" at zero, and Generate stays enabled. The server decides, and a 429 renders inline.
  - The probe's answer (past its sequence check) and a served letter each SET the pass from what came back: a `setPass(` whose argument is not the literal `null`. An answer read and then thrown away is the defect the probe exists to fix.
  - The probe's answer, a letter's success and a `monthly_limit` refusal each make the card known, and nothing else does: no other code in the card may call `setKnown(true)`. A failed probe never does, whether the failure lands in a `.catch`, a `.finally` or `.then`'s second argument: reading a failure as "no pass" disables a change at zero that the server may include. Nor does anything the probe runs before its request or outside its sequence check, and "inside" means inside the one answer callback that holds the check: its early return leaves only that callback, so a later `.then` runs for a dropped answer too.
  - A 5xx or a dropped letter leaves `known` as it was: `generate()` touches `known` only on its served path and inside its `isMonthlyLimit(e)` branch, never before the request, elsewhere in the catch or in a finally. If the tap dropped the probe's answer, marking the card known on that failure would read the unread pass as none. The 5xx-gated decrement (check-mirrors 30 P4) is unchanged.
  - Generate's `disabled=` is exactly `known && uses.out`. `!known || uses.out` names the same two words but disables while the card is unknown, and for good once a probe failed, uses left or not.
  - A sequence ref drops a probe answer that lands after a letter started. That answer describes the pass before the letter took its slot, and applying it would hide the next letter's cost. The probe takes its own number (`const at = ++seq.current`) before it asks, and the gate is read by its control flow, in one of two shapes: an early return on `seq.current !== at` (optionally `|| !alive`) before anything is applied, or the apply and `setKnown(true)` inside `if (seq.current === at) { … }` (optionally `alive &&`). The inversions, `=== … return`, `!== { apply }` and `alive` read the wrong way round, name the same words and drop every normal answer; an earlier draft of the check, which looked only for the operator, passed all three green.
  - The sequence check must also sit at the TOP LEVEL of the answer callback that applies. An early `return` leaves only its own function, and fires only when the conditions around it hold. So a check inside a nested arrow, under another `if`, or in an earlier `.then` than the apply lets a dropped answer through: every later `.then` runs whether the earlier one returned or not. A positive block under another `if` is refused as well. It adds a condition, just as an extra term in its conjunction would, and an answer that fails that condition is never applied and never makes the card known. The next draft of check-mirrors 36 read every `.then` as one text and counted everything after the return as guarded, so two refactors passed green. One moved `setKnown(true)` into a chained `.then`. The other filtered in one `.then` and applied in the next, which applies `setPass(null)` for a dropped answer, wiping a pass the letter just opened. In both, an answer the gate dropped still made the card known. That covers a late answer after a letter started, and also the old posting's answer after a posting change, which marked the new posting known before its own probe answered. Check-mirrors 36 now reads each answer callback on its own. A `setPass(` or `setKnown(true)` in any other answer callback counts as outside the gate. On an awaited probe, whatever follows the end of the block that holds the sequence check counts as outside too, because a `return` there may leave only a nested function.
  - Check-mirrors 36 pins each rule in this bullet on the real file and on 46 fixtures, each of which must go red on its own rule. Beside them sit 14 twins it must accept: the conjunction reversed and parenthesised, the shipped `!alive ||` early return, reversed operands, a braced return, the positive block with and without `alive &&`, an awaited probe in `try`/`catch`, a failure in `.then`'s second argument that leaves the card unknown, a `finally` that only clears loading, an earlier `.then` that only converts the answer, and the answer written as a `function` expression, as an `async` arrow with a bare parameter, or with a typed parameter and return type.
- **The fit ride is NOT read back, and a reload forfeits it.** This is recorded, not fixed, and on 2026-09-21 the owner ACCEPTED it as is: "check fit, then tailor" after a reload costs a second use, and nothing changes.
  - The ride's key is the analysed JD, which only `tailorStore` holds, and that store dies on reload.
  - After a reload, `startTailor` analyses the posting again through `/jd/analyze`. That is ANALYZE_JD at temperature, not JD_FIT, so the tailor carries another `jd_ref` and `claim_fit_ride` misses.
  - So "check fit, reload, tailor" costs 2 uses, and the "uses 1" note after the reload is TRUE.
  - **Offline it looks otherwise.** `StubClient` answers both tasks with `_stub_jd`, which ignores its input, so the stub claims a ride the real model forfeits. Never pin the reload case through the stub (the `open_fit_ride` docstring says so). The same artifact makes two stub kits one posting: their analysed JDs are identical, so one cover-letter pass covers both.

**The alert morning is refunded unless it emailed.**
- A scheduled morning reserves one `job_alert` use, and keeps it only when jobs were actually emailed. Nothing above the bar, zero matches, an all-filtered morning, a blank email, no SMTP, a failed send and a raise all give the use back — the owner's rule, and the honest one: a morning that mailed nothing did nothing for the user.
- **At an exhausted pool the morning does not run at all**: `last_skip = "monthly_limit"`, `last_run_at` untouched, no search. The card says so through `paused_reason` / `resumes_on`, which are computed from the pool on READ rather than from `last_skip`, so another feature spending the last use pauses the card immediately and a new month un-pauses it with no write.
- **`run_alert` never raises**, so one user's failure cannot abort the rest of the cron tick; and it may never be wrapped in `charged`, which would therefore never refund.
- **"Run now" is a search the user pressed**: its own daily cap, one `search` use, and it KEEPS the use once the search completed, because the jobs are in History whether or not the mail went. Before Phase 30 it had no cap at all, and it writes every scored match to History before the bar — an unlimited free ranked search behind a button.
- **The alerts cron now FAILS CLOSED**: gate on and `CRON_SECRET` empty is a 503 `cron_unconfigured`. A refunded free morning is only bounded to once a day while the Bearer check actually runs. All three crons fail closed. The nudges cron reaches no model and spends no use — which is why it USED to stay open — but it sends mail on the owner's own SMTP account, the resource the auth-mail budget protects and one this mail is not counted against, so an open URL is a mail trigger for anyone who guesses the path (Phase 30 review, SEC-4). Two sibling crons disagreeing about who may call them is worse than either rule, and `cron_nudges`' own docstring says all of this.

**The cross-lane contract, mirrored by hand and pinned.** `UsageOut`, `UsagePassOut`, the `monthly_limit` 429 detail, the `X-Uses-Remaining` / `X-Uses-Pass` headers and the one-entry-per-line `quota.FEATURES` literal are mirrored in `types.ts` and pinned by check-mirrors 32(j) and 32(b) — a field renamed on one side compiles green and reads `undefined` at runtime. Two per-posting readings ride the same contract (P30-RELOAD-PASS): `POST /cover-letter/pass` answers the whole-compared `UsagePassOut`, and `CoverLetterResponse.expires_in_s` sits in 32(j)'s names beside `included_until` and `changes_left`. The probe's path and its `{ jd }`-only body are pinned by check-mirrors 36(c). `FEATURES` is one quoted entry per line because the check parses it, and two entries packed onto one line are REFUSED rather than half-read.
- **`uses_meter` is a middleware declared ABOVE `access_gate`**, so it runs inside the gate and `request.state.user_id` is already the caller. A ledger write reaches the holder only for the user it was bound to, which is what keeps a cron request that charges many users from handing any of them a header. A ContextVar cannot be bound in a FastAPI yield dependency — the rule `llm_metering` already paid for.

**The knobs, and what each one costs.**
- **`FREE_MONTHLY_USES`** (10). `<= 0` switches the monthly limit off — the daily caps' kill-switch shape. The admin and plan `unlimited` are exempt and write no rows at all. **Changing it means hand-editing two copy keys in both locales** — `landing.hero.access` and `landing.faq.a3` in `frontend/src/locales/{en,he}/marketing.json`, four strings in all. Everything in-app interpolates the server's value, and **no privacy string carries the number at all** — it states the retention rule instead — so nothing else needs touching. **The measured worst case behind that 10:** about **$22 a month** per free account at `gpt-4o-mini` from the three uncharged daily-capped routes, plus up to **19 model calls** per forced late failure on `/jobs/match`, which is refunded (below).
- **The daily caps Phase 30 added**, each its own `usage_log` action, admins exempt, plan `unlimited` NOT exempt, `<= 0` disables one: `DAILY_UPLOAD_CAP` 10, `DAILY_JD_ANALYZE_CAP` 30, `DAILY_SEARCH_CONTEXT_CAP` 10 (these three are sub-steps of a counted flow and stay OFF the pool — counting them would make one tailor cost two uses), `DAILY_SCAN_CAP` 20 beside the scan's monthly charge, and `DAILY_FETCH_CAP` 60 on `/jobs/fetch`, which reaches no model and so is correctly off the pool but spends one outbound GET per call from the deployment's own IPs, and carried no cap of any kind until the review (COST-3).
- **`kits.MAX_REQUEUES` (2) is the only bound on re-running a kit.** A kit is paid for once at batch time and a requeue is deliberately never charged again — billing a user's daily cap for our own crash is what the refund rule refuses — so a pipeline that dies every time was an unlimited free re-run of it every 10 minutes, measured at 5 model calls a drain with the monthly count and the daily tailor count both unmoved (COST-4). After the last retry the next stuck run FAILS the kit with an error the user can act on and hands the use back. Pinned with its false-positive half: a kit stuck ONCE still gets its retry and still reaches `done`.

**Known open, recorded.**
- **A refunded alert morning is bounded only by the cron schedule** (COST-1): one search per enabled alert per day, at most 25 model calls, roughly $0.02-0.05 a month per enabled alert, and 0 on a repeat morning thanks to the score cache. Do NOT "fix" it by keeping the use whenever the search completed — that contradicts the owner's rule and charges people for mornings that mailed them nothing. If a tighter bound is ever wanted, the honest place is a daily `search` count in `run_all_alerts`, never inside `run_alert`, which Run now shares.
- **A late failure on `/jobs/match` refunds after up to 19 model calls** (COST-2), bounded only by the daily `llm` cap of 150 requests, which is never refunded: about $0.05-0.08 a day of the owner's money in the worst case. Phase 30 made this strictly better — before it the route took unlimited listings with no pool at all. The fix (keep the use once any listing was scored, the rule passes already follow) is deferred with the paid-tier revisit because it changes a pinned assertion and the blanket refund's shape.
- **Separate real mailboxes get separate pools** (disposable or catch-all domains), bounded by `SIGNUP_PER_IP` at 5 an hour — and cheaper now that a Google signup needs no mailbox round-trip. **No copy may promise "one free allowance per person".**
- **One interview use is up to 60 calls.** Since P30-PASS-SIZE every pass input is measured (the cover-letter tone is bounded at the schema instead), so the widest pass call (the scorecard) sends at most ~576 KB of client text (resume + job ad + the scorecard's transcript allowance), a chat turn ~544 KB, or the context window if smaller. The width of one use is its call count (60 in 3 hours; the daily `llm` cap bounds how many happen). Narrowing it, with fewer calls or a per-pass token budget, is an open owner decision, and either means a heavy honest session can cost 2 uses.
- **An installed extension older than 0.4.0**: at `monthly_limit` its autofill fills 0 answers and says nothing. It also keeps asking for every question, and each refused request spends a daily `llm` unit. 0.4.0 reads the refusal, stops and says why (the P30-EXT-LIMIT section below). Installed software cannot be changed by a deploy, so an older copy stays silent until its owner replaces the folder and reloads it. An open web tab now picks up the extension's spend when it is shown again.
- **A lost response keeps its use.** There is no client Idempotency-Key, by design — a reused key would re-run the model for free up to the daily caps — so a retry after a dropped stream pays again, the copy says so, and a finished search is in History whether or not its client was still listening.
- **The fit ride still lives only in page state, and that is accepted** (P30-RELOAD-PASS; owner decision 2026-09-21). A reload forfeits it, so after a reload "check fit, then tailor" costs 2 uses, and the "uses 1" note is TRUE. The owner accepted that second use as is; keeping the ride across a reload (jdText, jd, fit and checkedFor in account-scoped sessionStorage, a `tailorStore` change) was not taken. The cover-letter pass is off this list: since P30-RELOAD-PASS a remounted card reads it back through `POST /cover-letter/pass`. ### The extension reads its refusals, and a returning tab re-reads its uses (P30-EXT-LIMIT, 2026-09-21)

Up to extension 0.3 the assisted-apply autofill dropped every non-2xx from `POST /tools/screening-answer` (`if (!aRes.ok) continue;`). At a spent month it asked for every question anyway, counted only successes toward its 4-per-click cap, and ended on a green "Filled 2 fields" over empty question boxes. And no open web tab saw what the extension spent: the uses store's only writers were `/auth/me` on mount and the tab's OWN responses' headers.

- **A refused request is not free, so a refusal that would repeat stops the autofill.** `llm_user` counts a daily `llm` unit BEFORE `pass_charged` can refuse, and a daily count is never refunded (the charge-order rule above). The old loop therefore spent one daily unit per question per frame at a spent month, for nothing. `screeningRefusal(status, detail)` in `extension/popup.js` is the one reader. It STOPS on 429 `monthly_limit`, 429 `daily_limit`, 401 and every other client error except 400: each question carries the same resume and job text, so a 413 or a 422 repeats. It SKIPS only the question on a 400 or a 5xx. A fetch that throws stops too (`errNetwork`). The 4-per-click cap counts ATTEMPTS, refused ones included, so a page with one transient 502 drafts at most 3.
- **The refusal lives in the popup's status line and NEVER in the page.** The answer box belongs to an employer's form. That is also why a backend-only fix was rejected: the installed old build never reads a non-2xx body, and its one visible path writes `answer` verbatim into that box. A 200 whose `answer` said "you've used all your uses" would have been sent to an employer, and it would also have broken the ledger (a refusal counted as served) and the pinned 429 shape.
- **Green only when every question it asked about came back answered and reached the page.** A skip-class failure or an empty 200 turns the line amber (`warn`) with its own sentence. Answers already drafted for the frame are still written in before a stop. A frame that goes away before its drafted answers are written (the `fillScreeningAnswers` injection throws) turns it amber too, with `autofillAnswersUnwritten`, beside any refusal sentence. Writing FEWER than were drafted does not: `fillScreeningAnswers` leaves a box the user typed into alone on purpose, so that count says nothing about a failure. **The same carve-out hides one real failure**: a frame that re-renders IN PLACE while the answers are on their way (a React form moving to its next step drops the `data-jf-screen-q` tags) makes `fillScreeningAnswers` find no box, write nothing and return 0 without throwing, so the line stays green and never mentions the drafted answers. Telling that apart from a typed-into box needs the injected function to report the tags it could not find, a change to installed software that is not made here; `extension/README.md` says so to the user.
- **The month and the date come from `resets_on`, in the language of the messages Chrome loaded**, which a `uiLang` message in each `messages.json` states. It is never `navigator.language` or the client's clock. A French Chrome gets the English messages and must get an English month. It is the web app's `usedUpMonth` rule, and the sentences are `common.json`'s `uses.limitReached*` and `dailyLimit.llm`.
- **Nothing preflights on a count, in the popup or in a tab.** An open screening pass is shared with the web tool (keyed by feature alone) and still serves answers at 0 uses left. Smoke's "32.7 extension transport twin" pins that shape over the extension's own headers.
- **An installed copy does not update itself.** Before 0.4.0 it stays silent, and the manifest version on its `chrome://extensions` card is the only way to tell (0.3 never bumped it, so 0.2.0 means 0.3 or older). `extension/README.md` says so.
- **Showing a tab again re-reads its uses through `refreshUses(expectedId)`, never `getAuthMe`.** AppLayout listens for `visibilitychange` only once the guard has answered WITH a user, keeps that user's id, and asks at most once a minute (`shouldRefreshUses`, `USES_REFRESH_MS`), the mount's own `/auth/me` counting as the last ask. `getAuthMe` is the wrong call because it also re-stamps the resume draft's owner from every answer (`lib/draft.ts`, check-mirrors 30 F4). On a tab whose session expired while hidden, that would leave the unsaved edits owned by nobody, so they are never offered back after the next sign-in. With another account signed in from another tab, it would stamp this tab's resume with THAT account's id, and that account would be offered it as its own. `refreshUses` writes the uses store and nothing else, and only when the answer is the same account signed in (`usageIfSameUser`). It never forgets a stored code and never redirects; a session that ended still reaches AccessGate through the next protected call's 401. With the guard failed open, no id is known and nothing is registered.
- **Pinned by** check-mirrors 35 (a) to (d), which load the real `popup.js` into a node vm and EXECUTE the real `refreshUses` bundled out of `api/client.ts` (see `testing.md`), and by smoke's three "32.7 extension transport" checks. Those three turn `DAILY_LLM_CAP` back on around their own calls, because the block they sit in runs at 0 and `check_and_count` then records nothing. The first of them also pins `resets_on` to the bare `YYYY-MM-01` shape on its own, not only as equal to `quota.resets_on(...).isoformat()`: the popup reads only that shape, and a datetime would compare equal to the server's own function while every extension sentence fell back to the bare "this month" form.
