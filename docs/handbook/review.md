> pointer such as "CLAUDE.md's *Phase 22* section" (PLAN.md, code comments) resolves here. Every bullet records a

### The review — it points at the paper (2026-09-04)

- **`app/core/resume_review.py` is 26 deterministic checks with ONE home**, and it SUPERSEDES `ats_scan.py` and `resume_health.py` — both deleted, with their routes, their models, their two tool pages and their `health.*` / `ats.*` locale trees. Tools went from 8 to 6 here, and back to 7 in Phase 30, when the CV scan moved in from the public landing. Those two answered with a SCORE and a list of English sentences: a number the user optimises instead of a document they fix, and prose no Hebrew reader could read. A `ReviewFinding` carries a stable `id` — which IS the translation key, `doc.review.checks.<id>.{label,how}` — and a `path`, so the panel points at the block and says it in either language.
- **What it can say: this line is weak, this date is unreadable, this institution is template text. What it can never say: your resume is good.**
- **There IS a percentage now, and PLAN 28 item 1 said there would not be. The owner asked for one on 2026-09-04; this is how it stays honest.** The old rule — counts and evidence, because a count is checkable and a band is not — is kept in the only way that survives contact with a number: `reviewScore` is ARITHMETIC over the deterministic checks (`clean ÷ ran`), never a graded opinion, and every term in it is a count the panel also prints, so the reader can verify it by counting the rows. **`skipped` is excluded from the denominator**, which is the load-bearing half: counting unknowns as failures would drop the score for a reason the user cannot act on (no job attached), and counting them as clean is the defect `ReviewResult`'s three lists exist to prevent. `ReviewResult` still carries no score FIELD — the number is derived at the panel, so there is one definition and nothing stored that could go stale.
- **The "after" figure is a CEILING, not a prediction, and it is labelled "up to".** Clearing every listed check leaves every check that ran passing, so it is 100 by construction. It may never be phrased as a promise: fixing one thing can introduce another — measured in the browser, lengthening a bullet to add a metric tripped `bullet-long`.
- **"Each fix ≈ N%" is stated ONCE, beside the headline, and never as a per-row badge.** It rode on every row until an audit pointed out it is the same number on all of them BY CONSTRUCTION — `clean/ran` moves by exactly `100/ran` whichever check you clear — so a badge claiming to guide the reader to the best-value fix guided nothing, while spending ~35 px of a 333 px row on the primary viewport. A number that cannot vary is a sentence, not a column.
- **The row's LABEL is the jump target, not the crosshair.** It was a full-width `<button>` before the drawer and regressed to inert text with two 20 px icons at the far inline-end — on a phone, which has no hover, that left the panel's whole purpose reachable only through the smallest controls in the product. The label is a 208×32 button again and both icons are `h-8`, the floor `ThemeToggle`, the account avatar and this drawer's own close already keep.
- **Below `lg` the jump CLOSES the drawer, because the drawer is what the jump would land behind.** `jumpToBlock` switches the view, spotlights the block and scrolls to it — all three under an opaque full-bleed panel on a phone, with a 2200 ms spotlight the user cannot see start. The inline card this replaced sat ABOVE the document and never had the problem; the drawer reintroduced it and the breakpoint is evaluated at click time so rotation is handled.
- **It is modal below `lg` and beside the document from `lg`, and it says so to a screen reader.** `aria-modal` is bound to the breakpoint rather than hard-coded: under it the drawer and its backdrop cover the `z-30` tab bar and the paper, which is what makes the close button, the dismissable backdrop and the Escape key load-bearing; above it nothing is trapped and announcing a modal would be a lie. `overscroll-contain` sits on the `<aside>` (not only the scroller — the header row is outside it) and the backdrop is `touch-none`, the pairing `AppLayout` already documents having paid for once when the document scrolled behind a stationary sheet.
- **The hover tint sets NO geometry, and that omission is load-bearing.** At (0,3,0) the selector beats `Badge`'s `.rounded-full` (0,1,0), so a `border-radius: 4px` on it squared every skill and language CHIP the moment a row pointing at one was hovered — the pill snapped to a near-rectangle and back. Anything geometric added to that rule has the same reach.
- **`×N` is the MEASURED count, not the length of a truncated list.** `no-outcome` is capped at five findings server-side and carries the true number in `args.total`, under a comment saying a cap that hid the total would understate the problem — and the badge printed `×5` for nine bullets, doing exactly that. Reading `args.total` is scoped to that one check on purpose: `total` is not one quantity across the result, and `skills-unasked` sets it to a skills count.
- **check-mirrors 22 scrapes `doc.review.*` too, since 2026-09-04.** Its alternation was `(?:review|edit)`, so every scalar this drawer added — `upTo`, `why`, `showInDoc`, `close`, `nothingRan`, `eachWorth` — was guarded by nothing: check 27 covers `doc.review.checks.<id>.{label,how}` only, and check 8 stays green while a key is missing from BOTH locales. That is precisely how a raw key ships at 12 px in Hebrew.
- **A row is one CHECK, not one finding.** A first-draft CV produces five "no measured result" findings, and five identical rows saying the same sentence five times is what made the first version a wall of prose. The check is also the unit that CLEARS — fixing one of five bullets leaves it failing — so a per-finding row implies progress the document has not made. `badCount` counts checks for the same reason: a badge reading 17 over a panel showing three rows is a count nobody believes.
- **The explanation is behind an `ⓘ`, closed by default.** The how-text is the panel's most valuable copy and its most expensive: on screen for every row it buried the labels. `check-mirrors` 27 still requires `.label` AND `.how` in both locales — the toggle changes when the sentence is read, never whether it exists.
- **Three rules hold it together, and all three are smoke-pinned.** (a) **Every check always runs** — `findings ∪ passed ∪ skipped == CHECK_IDS` on every call, pairwise disjoint, so an empty result can never mean "you turned that one off" (a tool that ships three of four checks OFF manufactures a clean empty state that way). (b) **Unknown is never clean** — a check that COULD NOT run lands in `skipped`, never `passed`: a one-role CV has *no* gap history, which is not the same as a clean one, and `resume_health` reported `good` there. (c) **A clean resume produces ZERO findings** — no always-on advice, because a panel that always has something in it teaches the user to ignore the panel.
- **`POST /tools/review` carries NO `Depends`, and the module's inability to reach a model is the whole justification.** `useReview` re-runs it on a 400 ms debounce as the user types, so it must stay free. It is pinned off the **AST**, not by substring, because the module docstring NAMES `get_llm_client`, `urllib` and `datetime` in order to promise it does not use them and a grep cannot tell a promise from an import. It imports **exactly two** helpers from `scorer` (`_keyword_present`, `_tokens`) — a third name off that module is how the model gets a vote on an uncapped route with every substring pin still green. It takes an analysed `jd` or `null`, never `jd_text` (`extra="forbid"`, 422): the `/tools/ats-scan` cautionary tale, inherited deliberately.
- **`POST /tools/review/rewrites` is the one part that spends**, `Depends(llm_user)`, behind a button, capped at 5 bullets. Three guards run AFTER the call the way `check_fabrication` runs after a tailor: a `before` that does not match a bullet VERBATIM (that match is what yields the `path`, and "Use this" writes through it), a number in `after` that is not in `before` (reusing `structurer._extract_numbers`, so the two cannot disagree about what a number is), and a banned phrase (`voice_audit._BANNED_RE`). What they refuse is **counted and reported**, never swallowed. Patch **`review_rewrites.get_llm_client`**, never `app.llm.client`'s — the name is already bound, and patching the definition is a no-op that reads as a pass.
- **The block-path grammar is ONE grammar in two languages.** Python EMITS (`PATH_SHAPES`) and TypeScript RESOLVES (`BLOCK_PATTERNS`); check-mirrors 26 asserts set-equality and also compares `dkey`'s OPERATIONS on both sides. A shape only Python knows is a review row that jumps nowhere — listed, un-tappable, silently, on the surface whose entire promise is that it points. Every emitted index is smoke-pinned IN RANGE on the resume it came from: an off-by-one anchor renders identically and scrolls nowhere.
- **Five defects were found by writing the checks and watching them go red. Each is a guard that fired on legitimate input.** The acronym table deliberately omits **SQL and API** — nobody writes "application programming interface", so a pair whose long form no one writes puts an always-on row on every real CV (this list is inherited from `ats_scan` and was measured; do not widen it). **`ats_form` normalises `היום` to "Present"**, so comparing its output against what the user wrote flagged correct Hebrew on every Israeli CV with a current role — a current-date synonym is never a format defect. The **Hebrew weak-opener prefix walk bailed before it ran**, because `אחראי` does not itself begin with one of ו/ש/ה/ב/ל/מ. **`skills-sentence` passed on a resume with no skills**, which is unknown shown as clean. And **the gap check dropped ongoing roles entirely**, losing the START that anchors the gap before them — an ongoing span now reaches `_OPEN` (9999×12), which says "nothing after this can be unexplained" without reading the clock this module refuses.
- **Military service AND education both count as occupied spans.** A gap check that reads only `experience` reports the two or three years of service, and then the degree, as unexplained absence — the single commonest shape of CV in this app's primary market, told it has a hole in it. The finding anchors on the LATER span, `@mil.<i>` and `@edu.<i>` included.
- **A metric is a RESULT, not any digit.** `Python 3`, `Vue 3`, unit `8200`, `2 years` and `ISO 27001` are all scored as outcomes by a bare `\d`, which makes `no-outcome` useless in exactly the way that teaches a user to ignore it. It matches a number attached to a unit or a delta, en and he, and is capped at five findings with `args.total` carrying the true count.
- **…and a real result must not read as none either** (2026-09-23, PLAN 31.1/10). The review's own test CV was told "No measured result" on "duplicate refunds dropped to zero over 14 months", "Mentored 3 junior engineers" and "stock levels update in seconds instead of hours": a guard firing on legitimate input, the other half of the rule above. `_METRIC` now lets up to two words sit between a count and its unit (the unit list is unchanged, so `2 years` still matches nothing), and `_DELTA` gained three narrow shapes: a fall BY, TO or FROM an amount or zero ("dropped", "lowered", "decreased", "shrank", "fell"; "down to"), zero of a bad thing ("zero downtime"), and one time unit instead of another. Each is smoke-pinned beside a twin of the same shape that states no result and must stay flagged ("Dropped support for IE11", "Kafka instead of RabbitMQ", a count with no number). **`_DELTA` ends in one `\b` for the whole group**, so a new alternative must end on a whole word or a whole number (`\d+`, never `\d`): "by 30%" ending on "3" fails the boundary and matches nothing. The same quirk means the old `from … to \d` branch fires only on a single-digit end value, which is why a date range never matched it; that is left as is, since changing it would move other results.
- **A check skipped for lack of a job says so, by name** (2026-09-23). `skills-unasked` is skipped when there are no skills or no job, and on a resume with skills only the second is left; listed under "Couldn't check" by its finding label it read "Couldn't check: Mostly skills this job never asks for" on a review with no job. The panel now gives that case its own sentence (`doc.review.needsJob`, "Tailor for a job to also check which of your skills it asks for"), and every other skipped check keeps the "Couldn't check" line. Still shown, never dropped: unknown is never clean.
- **`args` values are `str | int` only.** A float renders as `3.0999999` in one locale and `3,1` in another, and this route is uncapped — so `length` formats its years as a string. An unmeasurable page count is **SKIPPED**, never swallowed as clean and never raised: a free uncapped route may not 500 on a renderer bug.
- **`_corpus` is the THIRD reader of the printed-section list**, after `scorer._resume_text` and the renderers, and it inherited that role from `ats_scan._resume_text`. The two corpora may differ in punctuation and case — `scorer` space-joins and lowercases for matching, `_corpus` newline-joins and preserves case for the acronym pairing — but they may NOT read a different set of sections, and a sentinel-per-section check pins exactly that.

### The drawer — the portal, the edge it opens from, hover, and the 390 px pass

- **It has two panes on a tailored draft** (2026-09-23, PLAN 31.3/3): "Changes" (the tailor's review, built by `TailorPage`) and this review, switched in the drawer's own header. It stays ONE drawer with every rule below, and its open state is a PANE (`DrawerPane`) the page can hold, since a draft's changes open from the toolbar and the summary line; `reviewOpen` is "any pane", the name check-mirrors 42's focus pin reads. `document-editor.md` has the pane's contents.
These bullets continue the review's list; in `CLAUDE.md` they had drifted under the *One clock, one frame* heading.

- **The panel is a DRAWER, portalled to `document.body`, and the portal is not tidiness.** An ancestor of `DocumentPanel` carries a `transform` (a motion wrapper mid-tween), and a transformed element becomes the containing block for every fixed-position descendant — so `position: fixed` resolved against a div two thousand pixels down the page. Measured before the portal: `top 162, bottom 3316`, pinned to neither edge, on a green build. `BlockEditSheet` and `Modal` portal for the same reason (so did `AccessGate`, until Phase 29 made it render nothing).
- **It opens from the INLINE-END edge** (`end-0`), so it is on the right in English and the left in Hebrew, and the slide-in keyframe reads `--drawer-from`, flipped to `-100%` under `[dir="rtl"]` — a hard-coded `translateX(100%)` slides the Hebrew drawer in from the far side, across the document it is about to sit beside. It animates **transform and opacity only**, never height (check-mirrors 11), and ends on its resting state so `prefers-reduced-motion` parks it on screen rather than off it.
- **`inset-block-start-14` and `inset-inline-end-0` ARE NOT TAILWIND UTILITIES.** Both were written, both compiled to nothing, and the build stayed green while the drawer floated unpinned in the middle of the page. The logical utilities are `start-*` / `end-*`; `top-*` has no logical twin and is used directly. This is the same failure mode CLAUDE.md already records for runtime-assembled arbitrary values: Tailwind generates from a SOURCE SCAN, so a class it does not recognise fails silently in the browser.
- **Hovering a row tints its blocks; it does not move them.** The sheet rule ("nothing on the paper moves on hover, on scroll, or on a timer") is about MOTION, and a flat background with no transition is a mark rather than an animation. `hoverPaths` is a SET because one row is one check and a check can flag five bullets — hovering "no measured result" lights all five, or the row points at an arbitrary one of them. Hover is an enhancement on top of the tap, never a replacement: a phone has no hover, and the tap still scrolls and spotlights.
- **Measuring the DOM after an `await` reads a DETACHED node.** React replaces the block element on the hover re-render, and `getComputedStyle` on a detached node returns defaults — which reported `rgba(0,0,0,0)` for a tint that was painting correctly, through four rounds of "specificity" debugging that had nothing to debug. Re-query after every await in a browser pass, and when computed style and a screenshot disagree, believe the screenshot.
- **The ≤390 px pass (2026-09-04, extended 2026-09-21), and the Chrome extension could not do it.** Its tab renders at a fixed viewport (measured: `innerWidth` stayed 1707 through an MCP `resize_window`, an OS-level `MoveWindow` to 600 px, and a re-navigation), so every "mobile" reading taken through it describes a 1707 px page. **Use Playwright for a real viewport** — `npx playwright@1.62.1`, `viewport: {width: 390, height: 844}`, `isMobile`, `hasTouch` — and set the language with `localStorage.setItem("lang", …)`, which is `i18n.ts`'s `lookupLocalStorage` key, not i18next's default. A fresh profile also gets the onboarding modal, so dismiss it or every screenshot is a picture of that instead.
- **2026-09-04:** **Measured at a real 390×844, both languages: no horizontal overflow open or closed (detector not recorded; see the 2026-09-21 trap below), the drawer covers the viewport and is `aria-modal` with a visible backdrop, every control is ≥32 px, and a row tap closes the drawer and lands its block in view.** Hebrew has zero row problems; English truncates one label (`Template text instead of a school ×2`), which `title` makes recoverable. The drawer's own close was the last 28 px control in the panel and is 32 px now: below `lg` it is the primary way out of a modal, so it may not be the smallest target in the thing it dismisses.
- **2026-09-21: what the second pass measured, and how.** Playwright 1.62.1 at 390×844 (isMobile, hasTouch, a fresh context per run), stub model, a scratch DB copy, four runs: {en, he} UI × {an English master, a synthetic Hebrew master}.
  - **The jump:** a row tap detaches the drawer in 31–57 ms. The block lands within 0.5 px of `scroll-padding-top + (innerHeight − scroll-padding-top)/2` (462 px), clear of the sticky toolbar and the tab bar, with the spotlight outline solid 2px at 0.8–1.5 s and gone at 2.6 s.
  - **The nonce:** a re-tap inside the 2200 ms window flips `block-settle-alt` to `block-settle`; once the window clears, the nonce starts again at 1 (`-alt`). An assertion that a later re-tap "flips" the name goes red on correct code.
  - **The dot:** it follows the PAPER, not the chrome. `::after` has `left: -10px` (block) / `-22px` (bullet) on an English CV and `right: -10px` / `-22px` on a Hebrew one, including Hebrew UI over an English CV and English UI over a Hebrew CV, and it stays inside `.sheet`.
  - **The badge:** absent (not 0) while the first review is in flight. It moves 439–487 ms after an inline COMMIT (blur/Enter, never per keystroke) and is still unchanged at +300 ms. It moves only on a `bad` check: a bullet edit moves the warn rows and the dot, never the badge.
  - **Coverage:** the drawer's controls are all ≥32 px; the pass covered the master document only.
  - **Known open, since fixed:** ~~the count is off-screen on first paint at 390 (the review pill is 5th in a self-scrolling row); every labelled tool pill is 30 px~~ (**fixed 2026-09-22**: the phone row leads with the review, the desktop rail keeps the shared order, and a labelled pill carries `min-h-8`; re-measured at 390 px, the review pill first and visible with its count in en and he, every pill 32 px; check-mirrors 44); and, for accessibility, the drawer took no initial focus: it is `aria-modal` below `lg`, yet after it opened `document.activeElement` stayed on the "Check my CV" pill outside it, in all four runs. **Fixed 2026-09-22** with `hooks/useDialogFocus`, which the drawer (only while it is open AND below `lg`: from `lg` it sits beside the paper and takes nothing) and `Modal` both call. On open, focus moves onto the container (`tabIndex={-1}`) unless something inside, such as an `autoFocus` field, already took it; Tab and Shift+Tab stay inside, and only the NEWEST open overlay handles Tab, so a modal opened over the drawer is not pulled back out; on close, focus returns to what held it before, only when focus is still inside or was dropped to `<body>` with the node. Measured with Playwright at 390x844, en and he: focus lands in the drawer and in the feedback modal, 0 of 25 Tabs and 0 of 25 Shift+Tabs leave either, and Escape and the close button hand focus back to the opener; with the hook turned off the same script read 25 of 25 leaving. **`BlockEditSheet` uses it too** (2026-09-22, the same day), and it is why the hook reads the opener in the RENDER that opens the overlay rather than in its effect: the sheet's first field has `autoFocus`, which takes focus during the commit, before any effect runs, so an effect-time read recorded that field as the opener and had nothing to hand focus back to. Measured at 390 px in en and he: opened from the keyboard on `@exp.0`, focus lands on the first field, 0 of 20 Tabs leave, and Escape returns focus to `@exp.0`. Pinned by check-mirrors 42, which also requires the opener read to come before the effect.
- **`document.documentElement.scrollWidth` cannot see horizontal overflow below `lg` in this app.** `html { overflow-y: scroll }` stops body's `overflow-x: clip` from propagating to the viewport, so body clips in place. Measured: a 600 px probe left `documentElement.scrollWidth` at 390 while `body.scrollWidth` read 600, and under RTL the overflow is on the LEFT (probe at −210). Detect overflow with `body.scrollWidth` plus an element sweep of both edges that skips descendants of their own scrollers, and probe it red with an injected wide div before trusting a green.
